# Scope and provenance

## Comparison with the problem

Daniel Carter's Problem 29 asks for a triangle-free construction, deterministic or random, with a witness for each disjoint positive and negative set of total size at most k when the positive set is independent. The Lean theorem supplies a deterministic finite graph specification for every natural k. The opening question in the workshop statement motivates this finite extension problem in terms of first-order properties of the Henson graph. The formalized theorem covers the finite graph assertion. The archive contains no Lean theorem about a probability distribution, first-order convergence, or the Henson graph's pseudofiniteness. [2026 Barbados Graph Theory Workshop, Problem 29, pp. 11–12](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf#page=11)

- Mathlib's SimpleGraph makes adjacency symmetric and irreflexive. TriangleFree quantifies over every ordered triple and rules out its three edges. A Fintype value supplies finiteness.
- Extension quantifies over all finite vertex subsets. On the finite carrier these range over every vertex subset. Its bound uses the sum of the two cardinalities and includes smaller and empty queries. It imposes independence only on A. B can induce arbitrary edges, and the premise places no restriction on edges between A and B.
- The witness is outside A and B, complete to A and anticomplete to B. The outside condition is explicit in the theorem, including for A empty. The empty query forces a vertex, so an empty graph cannot satisfy the statement. There is no lower bound on the input size or the graph order in the hypotheses.
- The carrier is chosen separately for each k. The theorem covers k = 0 as well. At k = 4 the formal construction has 40 address/value pairs per slot, 405 fields per record, and order at most 406^405. The initial handwritten construction allowed 80 pairs per slot and 725 fields. The formal theorem uses the smaller uniform capacity 3k(k−1)+k.
- Every numbered field is reachable, including another pair's address and expected value. Theorems address_range, address_surj, and address_injective prove the numerical range and uniqueness. Zero is the unused address. The finite sums keep the root, branches, addresses, and expected values distinct.
- Both directions of a failed adjacency receive certificates. Block.sound shows that a certificate rules out an entire slot. The mask uses the source's fields in one direction and its target fields in the reverse direction. Good.blocks includes ordered pairs of active slots with equal indices. Equal target colors are covered by the color certificate. Negative adjacencies are excluded in both directions in extension.
- The raw graph and its vertex predicate are defined before the extension theorem. The proof constructs a record, establishes its membership, and proves its adjacencies. No existence premise on suitable graphs is assumed. Classical choice selects finite witnesses. The proof source has no custom axioms, placeholders, native decision calls, or unsafe declarations. The numerical identity for 405 fields uses Lean's ordinary kernel-checked decide.

## Mathematical antecedents

Gregory Cherlin formulated the finite homogeneity and extension problem in “Combinatorial problems connected with finite homogeneity,” Contemporary Mathematics 131, and returned to it in “Two problems on homogeneous structures, revisited,” Contemporary Mathematics 558. [Author's bibliography, items 48 and 98](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~cherlin/Paper/older.html)

Chaim Even-Zohar and Nati Linial studied finite graphs with the three-vertex extension property, including Albert graphs, hypercube constructions following Erdős and Pach, and twisted constructions. Their introduction explicitly describes the outside witness. Shoham Letzter and Julian Sahasrabudhe bounded the possible extension parameter as a function of graph order. These are historical antecedents. The proof in this archive implements the record-and-slot construction developed for this problem, and invokes no result or code from either paper. [Even-Zohar and Linial](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5637) · [Letzter and Sahasrabudhe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08817)
